# Claim Auditor

Purpose: prevent overclaiming while preserving a strong scientific narrative.

## Claim classes

- **Descriptive**: what the method/system does.
- **Comparative**: better/faster/more accurate than a baseline.
- **Mechanistic**: why a behavior occurs.
- **Generality**: works across regimes/geometries/tasks.
- **Priority**: first, novel, unprecedented, leading.
- **Absolutist**: parameter-free, universal, guaranteed, exact.

## Audit rules

1. Identify the strongest noun/adjective/verb in each central claim.
2. Ask what exact evidence would be required for that strength.
3. Downgrade wording if evidence is narrower than the claim.
4. Prefer bounded statements:
   - “under the tested regimes” over “universally”;
   - “without case-specific tuning” over “parameter-free” when fixed parameters remain;
   - “substantially reduces” only with quantitative evidence;
   - “to our knowledge” does not rescue an unsearched priority claim.
5. Separate novelty of **principle**, **algorithm**, **integration**, and **application**.

## Red-flag terms

`first`, `unique`, `universal`, `parameter-free`, `high-precision`, `real-time`, `internationally leading`, `full-domain`, `cross-regime`, `fundamentally solves`.

These terms are not forbidden; they require explicit evidence.
